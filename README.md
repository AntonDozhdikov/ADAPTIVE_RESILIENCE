The study examines the sociopolitical resilience of Russian society, including its stable and adaptive components. 
The empirical basis is provided by data from the Public Opinion Foundation: studies of social sentiment and expectations for 2017–2026, 
the "Anxieties and Concerns of Russians" survey with comparable dynamics for 2015–2026, and the 2026 "Image of Russia" survey, 
used for independent verification of the results. 
The analysis encompasses respondents' expectations regarding the development of the situation around the country and their own families over 6–12-month and 3–5-year horizons.
It includes reasons for anxiety at the individual, national, and global levels, as well as differences between social groups. 
The following hypotheses were tested: nonlinear dynamics of social expectations, more favorable assessments of the future with respect to family compared to the country, 
differences between the near and distant horizons, the relative constancy of anxieties and expectations, and the existence of stable "group anxiety profiles." 
The following approaches were used for testing: trend assessment, precise identification of structural shifts using the penalized dynamic programming method, 
permutation tests, correction for multiple comparisons, cluster analysis, principal component analysis, and cluster robustness testing on repeated samples.
No sustained linear increase or decrease in social expectations was detected in 2017–2026 (the relationships are likely more complex and nonlinear). 
The most pronounced change in the time series structure occurs in 2022–2023. 
In all comparable waves, expectations regarding one's own family are higher than expectations regarding the country; estimates of medium-term prospects for 3–5 years are also higher than short-term ones.
After correction for multiple comparisons, no statistically stable relationship between the dynamics of anxieties and expectations was found.
Cluster analysis revealed slightly different adaptation profiles of different social groups. 
An independent review of the "Image of Russia" study confirmed six consistent correlations, primarily in terms of overall positive expectations and assessments of Russia as a developed and prosperous country. 
The results demonstrate that the sociopolitical stability of society in the study population is combined with variability in individual indicators and cannot be characterized by a single constant. 
The most significant differences are between personal and national assessments, as well as between immediate and distant time horizons.

The study was conducted using open and officially published data from Russian sociological sources.
